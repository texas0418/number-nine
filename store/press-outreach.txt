THE RULE ON DEVICE 6, because this is where the email earns its keep.
You may name it. Nominative use in press conversation is fine and is decided
policy (MARKETING.md, 2026-07-27). What you must never write in your own
voice: "spiritual successor", "the sequel Simogo never made", "from the world
of", or anything implying they endorsed, blessed or are connected to this. The
difference is simple. Your feelings about their game are yours to describe.
Their mark is not yours to borrow. If a reviewer draws the comparison
themselves, that is theirs to print and you can quote it afterwards.

NEVER in any email: a puzzle method or answer, a frequency, her hour, or any
claim of screen-reader support. The press kit already discloses that there is
none, which is the correct way to handle it.

I Waited Thirteen Years for Another DEVICE 6, Then Built One Myself

BODY

Hello <NAME>,

DEVICE 6 came out in 2013 and I have never really got over it. I kept
expecting another, from them or from anyone, and it never came. So I spent the
last stretch building the thing I wanted to read.

Number Nine is a horror novella for iPhone in six chapters, one a night. A man
inherits his estranged brother's house on the marsh, a war-surplus shortwave
receiver, and nineteen years of listening logs written in a hand that never
wavered. On the fourteenth of June the entries stop mid-sentence. There is a
station where none should be, and a woman counting in groups of five who has
been counting for nineteen years.

The text is the architecture. It turns when the room turns, it mirrors when
she speaks, and you turn the phone in your hands to follow it. Fifty locks
stand between the first page and the last and not one of them is a quiz or a
multiple choice. There is also a cryptogram every night, free forever, the
same one for every player on earth, and a year of them in order tells a second
story underneath the first.

=========================================================================
FINDING THE RIGHT PERSON
=========================================================================
This matters more than the outlet. A pitch to a named writer who covers horror
or narrative games gets read. A pitch to tips@ usually does not.

1. Search the site for recent reviews of small narrative or horror games.
2. Take the byline. Find that writer on Bluesky, Mastodon or their own site.
3. Most publish a contact address or say plainly what they want pitched.
4. Only fall back to the generic address if there is no byline to be found.

Do NOT paste a list of addresses from a media-contact database. They are stale,
they get you filtered, and one email to the right person beats fifty to the
wrong ones.

=========================================================================
RULES OF THE THING
=========================================================================
- One follow-up, seven days later, three lines maximum. Never a second.
- Personalise the first sentence or do not send it. A visible mail-merge is
  worse than silence.
- Send them individually. Never a group email, never a visible CC list.
- Expect most to go unanswered. That is the normal rate and not a verdict on
  the game. Ten thoughtful emails beat a hundred copied ones.
- If someone covers it, thank them and do not argue with the score.
- Never offer a code in exchange for coverage or a review. Offering a free
  code so someone CAN cover it is standard and fine. Conditioning it on
  coverage is not, and it is the thing that ends relationships with writers.

SUBJECT

An iPhone-Only Horror Novella That Re-Typesets Itself at Every Dynamic Type Size


BODY

Hello Federico,

<ONE TRUE SENTENCE ABOUT SOMETHING HE RECENTLY WROTE, OR DELETE THIS LINE>

DEVICE 6 came out in 2013 and I never got over it. Another one never arrived,
from Simogo or anyone, so I spent the last stretch building the thing I wanted
to read. It went on the App Store this week.

Number Nine is a horror novella in six chapters, one a night. The premise is a
numbers station that has been counting for nineteen years after everyone who
was supposed to be listening stopped. But the reason I am writing to you is
how it is built rather than what it is about.

It is iPhone only and it is not a port of anything. The text is the
architecture: prose runs down the page when a character goes down stairs and
climbs back up when they return, it mirrors when the voice on the station
speaks, and the reader physically turns the device to keep reading. That only
works if the typesetting is real, so the whole book re-typesets at every
Dynamic Type size including the largest accessibility ones, and the rotated and
mirrored passages re-flow with it rather than breaking. Getting that to hold at
the biggest sizes took longer than writing two of the chapters.

Some of the locks use the instrument rather than a keypad: the magnetometer,
the gyroscope, screen brightness, haptics. Every one of them also accepts touch,
so nothing depends on physically moving the phone.

The parts I suspect you will care about:

  No account, no server, no sign-in. It is entirely on-device SQLite and the
  whole book works in aeroplane mode.
  One payment, never a subscription. First chapter free in full.
  No ads, no tracking, no analytics SDK, no crash reporter. The privacy label
  is one line: Purchases, not linked to identity, not used for tracking.
  No notifications, no timers, no energy meters, no daily-login anything.
  Portrait locked and dark only, both on purpose.

There is one thing it does not have and I would rather tell you than have you
find it: no VoiceOver support. It is not built. Dynamic Type is thorough and
every hardware puzzle has a touch alternative, but screen reader support is
honestly not there, and I did not want to imply otherwise.

There is also a free part that never expires: a new cryptogram every night, the
same one for every player on earth, generated from the calendar date. A year of
them in order tells a second story a night at a time. It costs nothing and
needs no purchase, so it is a fair way to see whether the thing is any good.

Press kit and assets: https://numbernine.simonbuilds.app/press/
App Store: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/number-nine/id6796237101

Happy to send a code that unlocks the whole book, no strings and nothing
expected in return.

Simon Shih
support@simonbuilds.app
